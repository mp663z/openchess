# T0271 Store/corruption/fuzz/fault

## Normative requirements

1. The battery is deterministic and seeded. It fuzzes the shipped production corruption scan (`store.corruption`, T0269). Every input log is built from an independent model of data/contracts/wal.yaml and data/contracts/corruption.yaml, then corrupted at a known position. The model restates the entry-id derivation, the type-tagged length-framed canonical encoding, the quarantine token and the scan-id derivation in the test and never takes them from production. The contract reference engine (`tests.test_t0266_corruption_contract`) is the differential oracle for every case.
2. The eight generators produce 341 cases:
   - `happy` (64): random logs with a corrupt suffix of 0-3 entries and a loss bound at or above the loss.
   - `tear` (42): all 14 WAL-invalid but in-domain corruption shapes, followed by honest-looking entries.
   - `loss` (24): a loss over the bound is `excessive_loss` with no sink call, a loss exactly at the bound salvages, and a clean log with a zero bound is clean.
   - `request` (32): 16 malformed requests, over a clean-domain log and over an out-of-domain log. They include renamed, missing, extra and non-ASCII keys, str-subclass and colliding keys, a dict subclass, list or None request, and a bool, int-subclass, float, str, None, list or negative max_loss.
   - `container` (6): non-list and list-subclass logs.
   - `domain` (72): 6 in-domain values placed at the corruption point or after it, and 20 out-of-domain values placed there or inside a verified entry. Depth and int bits are placed exactly at MAX_DEPTH and MAX_INT_BITS and one past them.
   - `sink` (62): a forged `CorruptionError` of every class, a forged `WalError` of every class, six BaseException kinds and 15 bad outputs, plus a scribbling sink. The bad outputs are None, bytes, int, list, str subclass, upper hex, a trailing newline, a leading space, another prefix, a flipped digit, a surrogate, and the token of the empty, a shorter, a longer and a reversed suffix.
   - `live` (39): a sink edits the caller's live log and request, then answers honestly, raises or returns a flipped token.
3. Every case holds against production. There is no raw escape: failures are only a typed `CorruptionError` with its mapped code. The outcome is the independently known one and the same as the contract reference. At the sink boundary the error is FRESH, never the forged object. The sink is called exactly once per salvage with the canonical suffix, and never when the log is clean or the scan rejects before quarantine. The request is identical afterwards at every level. A rejected scan leaves the log identical too. A salvage keeps exactly the verified prefix, in place by identity and restored by value, and returns the receipt in contract order.
4. Outcomes are deterministic by value, rejections do not poison a later scan, and scanning a salvaged log is clean.
5. Mutation check: each of the 32 one-guard source mutants of store/corruption.py fails the corpus, with `CorruptionError` and `_fail` rebound to the production class.
   - Boundary mutants: except Exception, CorruptionError pass-through for all classes and for each class, and WalError pass-through for all classes and for each class. They are pinned in `FORGE_TARGETS` to their exact forged-case label and must fail exactly that case.
   - Sink handling: isinstance sink output, no token equality, a shared frozen suffix, no restore of the log or of the request, and a sink call on a clean log.
   - Request and loss checks: isinstance request or max_loss, no request length check, no request key guard, no max_loss minimum, an inclusive or missing loss bound.
   - Domain: inclusive depth or int bits, no alias check, and no surrogate check.
   - Salvage and receipt: a bisection that keeps a bad midpoint, the commit keeping one entry or missing, and the scan id swapping the counts.
   - The identity mutant is green. Five equivalent edits are asserted green: `search` for the token grammar and no token grammar (both backed by token equality), no UTF-8 check on output (backed by the grammar), and isinstance key or log checks (backed by the exact-type dispatch in `_encode`).

## Requirement-to-test mapping

- R1, R2, R3: `test_fuzz_cases_hold` (one per generator), `test_corpus_reaches_every_class_and_shape`, `test_every_boundary_error_class_is_forged`.
- R4: `test_determinism_by_value`, `test_no_poisoning_after_rejections`.
- R5: `test_identity_source_mutant_is_green`, `test_mutant_is_red`, `test_equivalent_edits_stay_green`.
